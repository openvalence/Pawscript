# Tracking

The Track tab runs a tracker over a range of the open video and turns the motion it sees into points. Each finished run is kept (drawn as a **ghost** in the lanes) and is at once the script of the shots it covers: it is **live**, drawn on the stroke lane, played by the Machine and the device, written by Publish. You fix its direction and depth per shot and every fix shows at once. **Bake** (`Shift+B`) makes a shot's points permanent, so no later run or fusion change moves them; a hand edit bakes the shot it touches, in the same undo step; **Unbake** gives a shot back to its run.

Position convention: pos 0 = deepest / fully in, 100 = out. Trackers only see geometry, so they do not know which end is "in", the stroke axis and the flip tools are how you tell them.

## Requirements

- `uv` on PATH (docs.astral.sh/uv). The app starts the tracker as `uv run --project trackers --extra sam2 --extra describe pawtrack`; set `PAWTRACK_CMD` to point it elsewhere. The first start may install packages, so the first tracker list can take a while (it gives up after 120 s; press Retry).
- Optical-flow trackers (`lk_box`, `template`, `lk_two_point`) run on the CPU. `sam2_mask`, `tapnext_points` and Describe need a CUDA GPU and the matching extras (see "GPU notes"). `cotracker_points` is not included (non-commercial license, see [CoTracker3](cotracker.md)).

## Track a range

1. Open the video, switch to the Track tab (Ctrl+2).
2. Set a range: I at the start, O at the end. No range = the whole video. "Scene at playhead" in the Range section sets the range to a detected scene; "Whole video" clears it.
3. Paint the parts you want followed with Groups (Tab), on a good frame (or paint nothing: the grid covers the whole frame). Nothing is drawn on the video: no box, no clicks.
4. For a points tracker, draw the stroke axis (X, see below).
5. (Just one part, its size: Track ROI, Ctrl+T, see "Track just one part".)
6. Press **T**. The video follows the tracker frame by frame; the stroke lane shows the live signal; the status bar shows progress and a cancel. Esc cancels.
7. When it finishes, the run is selected, appears as "#n <tracker>" under **Runs**, and a toast gives its point count. Its shots play it at once (live). New runs that can fuse start with **Smart stroke** on.
8. **T** again on a shot that already has a run of the same tracker replaces that run (the toast says "replaces the earlier run") and takes over its shot settings: Smart stroke on or off, camera, source, strokes, depth, fusion, shaft marks. Track › **Track as a new run (keep the earlier run beside it)** keeps both, to compare. Runs of another tracker, or reaching past the new span, stay.

**Paint on the best frame; the scene seeds there.** The painted parts remember the frame they were painted on. The points trackers (`cotracker_points`, `tapnext_points`) lay their points on that frame of the scene that contains it and track both ways from it, so scrub to a frame where the part is fully in view (full penetration, nothing cut off by the frame edge) before you paint: a scene that opens on a close-up or an empty frame no longer gets its grid laid on a bad frame. Other scenes of the range seed on their first frame (param `seed_frame` = `first`) or on a frame picked from the scene's thumbnails (`auto`). On an orbit / turntable shot or a fast pan the points keep being re-seeded into the voids (also as soon as the picture has traveled one grid cell); if the run still loses the part, paint it again on a frame where it is fully in view and press T. The run's decision record (Why) shows the frame each scene was seeded on.

**Camera moves and continuing scenes are soft cuts: the run goes on through them.** Where the action carries on across a scene boundary (a camera split from Detect scenes, a scene marked *continues*) or the camera starts or stops moving inside a scene (a pan, zoom, turntable or handheld stretch in the scene's camera segments), Track sends a *soft* cut instead of a hard one. At a soft cut the tracker does not start over from your box: every point keeps its number, its group color (g1 stays g1) and its seeded group (penis / balls / hips keep their names), and the tracker tops up at that frame: points it has lost are replaced at once, empty parts of the picture and of your box get new points, and a seeded group whose part is back in view but whose points were lost is laid again on the part, under its old name. The number of points stays what you asked for (TAPNext 512 / 1024 included): lost and off-frame points make room first, a point it can still see is never dropped, and gaps it has no free points for wait for the next second's top-up (raise `max_points` to let it grow instead). At a real cut (a new shot) it still starts over from your box.

**A pan no longer drowns the strokes.** Each stretch between soft cuts is scaled to its own range, so a pan's travel no longer sets the range for the still part of the shot, and inside a stretch where the camera moves (or the tracker sees the background travel) and the strokes have shrunk to ripples on the pan, the camera's drift is taken out before scaling: from the tracker's own camera estimate when it is reliable, else as a smooth trend over one stroke. The thrusts keep their size in the lane during the pan, and there is no jump where the stretch begins or ends. The Why section lists every stretch ("segment 2 (4.0–10.1 s): pan → camera trend removed 38 px (camera path; strokes 0.12 → 0.91 of the range)"). To get the old behavior, set `pipe_cam_detrend` off.

## Track just one part (Track ROI)

When the script is just how big one part reads over time (the penis's visible length, say) and there is nothing to measure it against:

1. Paint the part with Groups (Tab) on a frame where it is fully in view; close the tool (Tab) or leave it open on that part.
2. Press **Ctrl+T** (or **Track ROI** on the shot chips). One part painted in the range: it is tracked at once. With the Groups tool open on a part: that part, no question. Several: a menu at the pointer asks which part (the one under the pointer, else the one you tracked last on that shot, first; the part the menu points at is outlined on the video; Enter or a click picks, Esc cancels; its last row tracks every painted part with the picked tracker, as before; a tracker that takes no painted parts, such as Optical flow (box), says it tracks one part at a time). Nothing painted: it says so and offers to open Groups.
3. The run covers the shot the part was painted on (inside the selection or the selected action region) and lands as any run: on the lane, its range taken, a run of the same tracker over that span replaced, selected, the painted frame marked on the timeline. Ctrl+T again replaces it.

The tracker: the one picked in the Tracking panel when it takes a box and is not the grid's point tracker (Optical flow, for instance), else **SAM 2.1 mask**: the painting is its seed mask and the run's signal is the part's size against the painted frame. Change what is measured afterwards in the run's controls (source, against, depth). Trackers that need a box get the painting's bounds (10 % added each side). With no GPU, SAM is off and Optical flow (box) follows the bounds on the CPU.

Old projects: runs made with a drawn box load, play and re-derive as they were. With no part painted, Ctrl+T on a selected old box run re-tracks it with its stored box (a toast says so).

**Scenes contain shots.** A scene is the act (who, where, what, modifiers, groups, fill): tag it once. A shot is a camera span inside it (a cut, a pan, a zoom, an internal view, an off-screen or blank stretch): its view and camera chips are its own. The Scenes row has two strips on one time axis. The upper **Scenes** strip shows one rounded block per scene with a gap between neighbors: its number and its act in words ("3 · anal penetration · knotting"), tinted by the act's color, modifier stretches as thin lines inside it, its fill or offers as short words; a dashed empty block says "untagged". The lower **Shots** strip shows that scene's shots, numbered in it (3.1, 3.2), each with its camera glyph, its view as a fill (hatched = off-screen, dotted inset = internal view, black = blank), a line along its bottom for its tracked state (solid = baked, dashed = live: it plays its run, green = filled or bridged, dotted gray = generated, none = not tracked yet) and a "?" when groups found again after a cut wait for your Accept. Three boundaries, three looks: a **scene boundary** is the gap above and a strong line below; a **cut** (a hard cut between shots of one scene: the tracker reseeds, the groups carry on by name) is a thin solid line with a notch; a **camera move** (the same take: tracked through, same points and channels) is a dotted line with no notch. The lanes below repeat them quietly (a faint line at each scene boundary, short ticks at a cut, a dotted tick at a camera move; right-click the row to turn the scene lines off). Zoomed out, the shots strip keeps only the boundaries. The "?" at the row's right edge shows this legend; hovering anything says what it is.

**Modifiers: mark an orgasm (or knotting, throbbing, idle) without splitting anything.** The bottom **Modifiers** strip of the Scenes row holds them as bars in their color, free inside their scene: a span may cross shots, be a few frames long, and a scene may have several. The **orgasm (spurting)** span is where the shots happen: **Detect cumshots** looks inside it and the spurt markers (U) belong in it. An **idle** span marks a stretch where nothing happens (talking, posing, repositioning) inside an act: it is held instead of tracked there (the tracker replaces the points with a hold); one covering a whole shot makes that shot idle (not tracked, held at rest, Autorange's rest band), exactly like the Idle structure chip on a scene, which is the same thing as an idle span over the whole scene. Ways to mark one:
1. **Draw it**: drag along the Modifiers strip from where it starts to where it ends; a small menu at release lets you change its kind (it starts as the kind you used last).
2. **Live, at the playhead**: **Shift+O** where the orgasm starts, **Shift+O** again where it ends (play and press; **Shift+K** does the same for knotting; Esc forgets a pending start).
3. **Over a range**: with a range set (I / O, a loop, or the selected shots), the **orgasm** / **knotting** / **throbbing** chip (Scene chip popover) or **Alt+Shift+O** / **Alt+Shift+N** marks exactly that range.
4. **From the playhead**: with no range, the chip or key starts a 4 s span at the playhead (inside its scene) that you then drag; pressed where one is, it removes it.

Edit a span on the strip: drag its ends to resize (snaps to cuts and spurt markers, always onto frames; Shift: no cut / spurt snapping), drag its middle to move it (everything that reads the span follows while you drag: the Range band, the curve, the panels, and a Knotting effect made over a knotting span with Alt+N, which moves and resizes with it until you move or resize the effect itself on the effects row; spurt markers stay on their frames; Esc while dragging puts it back; the whole drag is one undo step), click to select (Shift adds, Alt+drag box-selects); with the row focused **G** moves, **S** scales, **Shift+D** duplicates and **X** / Delete removes the selected spans. Right-click a span for its kind, **Detect cumshots over this orgasm**, or delete. A span stops at its scene's boundary (dragged past it, it is clipped; one marked across a boundary gives each scene its part). Splitting, merging or re-cutting scenes never loses a span: their times are fixed, a merge keeps both scenes' spans. The Scene chip popover lists the scene's spans with their times (click one to select it on the strip and go there). An orgasm the tagger detected shows as a dashed bar over its whole scene until you mark one by hand there. Old projects: a modifier that covered a whole scene becomes a span over exactly that stretch. Selecting works on shots: click a shot in the Shots strip to select it (click a scene in the Scenes strip to select the whole scene), Shift+click selects every shot from the active one to the clicked one (across scene cuts too), Ctrl+click adds or removes one shot, a drag along the row box-selects the shots it touches (Shift adds, Ctrl takes away), and double-click (or Alt+click) selects the whole scene. A toggles all / none (Alt+A none, Ctrl+A all, Ctrl+I the others; B box select, C circle select) while the row has the keyboard. The range follows the selection (its first shot's start to its last shot's end), so playback, the loop and Track work on it. Selected shots are tinted, the active one outlined, a scene with only some of its shots selected outlined dotted. Everything acts on the selection, and the chips say what they will hit ("Scene 2 · shots 2.1 to 2.3", "3 shots in Scene 2", "Scenes 2 to 4"): view and camera chips and Same take on every selected shot; where / what / modifier / structure chips and Fill on every scene that has a selected shot; Track on exactly the selected shots. Right-click the row for the selection's own edits (Make these shots their own scene, Merge the selected shots into one, Join the selection into one scene, Same take as the shot before), for Split scene here / Split shot here and Merge with next / previous scene or shot, the scene's Fill, **Stabilize**, and **Add effect** (Knotting by the same span rule as Alt+N, at the click; Unknotting; Spurt; More effects). Delete / X in the row removes the cuts in the selection (two or more shots merge; one shot merges into the shot before it). Double-click a boundary to merge across it. Drag a scene boundary in the Scenes strip to move it to another cut (the shots between change scene); drag any boundary in the Shots strip to move it in time; right-click a boundary to make a cut a camera move, a scene boundary, or back. Alt+Shift+K splits the scene at the playhead, Alt+Shift+M merges it with the next (the shot commands have no keys: K is the shuttle stop). A merge keeps the first scene's tags and groups; the second's are kept as an offer (Actions panel and right-click: Accept / Drop; splitting the scene again at that cut gives them back). Detect scenes makes shots and joins camera moves into one scene. Old projects open migrated: each old scene is a shot, and camera splits, "continues" links and neighbors with the same where / what become one scene, with nothing lost. A signal pick on one shot (a group, a part, a relative channel) reaches the scene's other shots on the same run; proj / proj_cam / pair stay per shot (each shot's camera picks them). The script's level carries across every cut inside a scene and resets at a scene cut. **Groups across a hard cut**: the groups belong to the scene, drawn on one frame; a take after a hard cut inside the scene (new framing, the parts elsewhere) gets them found again on its first frame before it is tracked, or when you press Suggest there (tracker v4.13 `suggest_groups` with `prior`: the tagger looks for the same parts, Bottom / Top swapped if the characters changed sides), under the same names, so `sem_c1_penis_*` is one series across the cut. They are dotted with a "?" until you Accept them (Groups toolbar: Accept re-found) or repaint them; a part not found is named in a toast. This needs the tagger on the GPU; without it the take is tracked with the tagger's own groups. Field table: docs/scenes-and-shots.md.

**Stabilized preview** (Shot chip **Stabilize**, menu Track > Advanced): the video plays with the camera's motion removed, from the camera path (`cam_x` / `cam_y`) of the run linked to the shot under the playhead (else the selected run, else a visible run there; points trackers only). The picture and its overlays move by minus the camera's drift, so the action should sit still and the frame edges move: what camera removal (`proj_cam`) works from. It resets at each cut (the path starts again per run scene). It follows the shot's camera settings (Adjust after tracking, below): a zoom or rotation is taken out when the shot's camera mode removes it. With no camera path there it says so and stays off.

**Tagged scenes are planned, not tracked blindly.** A range that spans an **Off-screen** scene is split: that scene is left out of every request (no run spans it, so the bridge over it stays) and the action pieces around it are tracked one by one with the panel's tracker, each linked to its scenes; a toast names what was left out. The panel's Scenes setting **one** tracks the range as drawn. For the whole video, `Shift+T` (**Track tagged scenes…**) lists the plan from the tags (off-screen skipped, internal views on SAM · area with their box, continuing scenes as one item, camera tags picking the signal, scenes with a run already done) and **Run** tracks it with one status-bar task; Esc once stops after the running piece, twice kills it. See workflow 7 in the workflows guide.

A Track from the panel aborts a camera check the panel started itself, then waits for a running score, a camera check another feature started, or a cancel that has not finished yet before starting; it refuses while trackers are being listed or scenes are being detected or described.

## The stroke axis (X)

One arrow on the video from the **OUT** end to the **IN** end. For the points trackers it is what gives `proj` a direction: moving toward the IN end = lower pos. Recommended workflow: click 2–4 points on the moving part, then draw the axis once.

1. Pick a tracker whose schema has `axis` (Track ROI's tracker); its raw axis params stay out of the panel.
2. Press **X** (Track > Draw stroke axis). Press at the OUT end, release at the IN end; or click twice. Esc cancels, Delete removes an existing axis; the ends can be dragged afterwards.
3. While the tool is on, a bar at the foot of the video (**Next run**) sets the mode: `auto` | `vert` | `horiz` | `arrow` (= custom). Drawing an arrow selects `arrow`; leaving it blank sends the tracker's default. Its readout shows angle, length in frame heights and what will be sent; **Clear** removes the arrow.

What is sent:
- Points trackers (`cotracker_points`, `tapnext_points`): `axis: custom` with `axis_x0..y1` from the arrow, and `primary: proj` if `primary` is still at its default.
- `lk_box` / `template` (no custom axis): the nearer of `vertical` / `horizontal`, with `pipe_invert` toggled when the arrow points up or right, so that toward IN is still lower pos.

The axis is stored per video and in the project (`tracking` block).

## Groups (Tab): paint what the tracker pins

The points trackers (`cotracker_points`, `tapnext_points`) pin a few groups of points on the seed frame and keep them for the
whole scene: by default the shot tagger's penis, balls and hips. The Groups tool lets you see what the tagger suggests and
paint your own groups instead, before you Track.

**Keys in the tool.** `Tab` opens and closes it (nothing else). While it is open, hold `Space` and move the mouse sideways to
scrub around the frame (Left / Right one frame, Shift finer; release: back on the painted frame). This is the one deliberate
exception to "Space is always play / pause": inside the Groups tool Space only scrubs (a press without movement does nothing;
the play button still plays); outside it Space plays and pauses as everywhere else.

**The bar.** The tool is one slim column of buttons at the stage's left edge (on a short stage it wraps into a second
column); the notes card, the context line and the first-run hint sit in a column at the right edge. None of it covers the
picture: the picture is fitted between the two columns, and on a small window the side panels fold while the tool is open
(they come back when it closes). The buttons: fold, keys and mouse, erase, circle to fill, grow, knot brush, brush size, edge snap, the character for new
groups, Suggest, accept all, copy from a similar frame, the **groups list**, notes, **Options** and close. The groups list
(its button shows how many groups there are) holds a row per group: click a row selects the group, right-click opens its
picker, **Shift+click a row in the groups list: strokes paint into that group**; the eye leaves a group out of Track, the
bin deletes it, a suggestion has its confidence and ✓. **Options**: ask for the part after each new group, fill an empty
take from a repeat, **Points per group** (auto: the groups share the box's points, each at least 16 and together at most
3/4 of them; above 0: that many extra points per group, more VRAM), the paint **margin** (how far inside a part's edge the
outline sits) and the seeder line. The **notes** card beside the bar says where the take's groups are and offers what fits
the frame on screen (Go there, Move them here, Copy groups, Split into groups ...); it opens by itself off the groups' frame.

1. Put the playhead on the frame you want to paint on and press **Tab** (or **Groups** in the lane toolbar or the tracker
   panel). **The tool never moves the playhead**: a take without groups is painted on the frame on screen (a shot's
   first frame stays that shot's, even when the cut list's times are a hair after the frame's), and that frame becomes the
   take's seed frame (the tracker seeds the take there and tracks both ways). A take that already has groups keeps their
   frame: anywhere else in the take they draw ghosted (dashed, no fill) and the notes card says **This take's groups are on
   frame N** with **Go there** (the only seek the tool makes, on your click) and **Move them here** (they are re-stamped onto
   the frame on screen, outlines as they are, one undo step). In the groups' own shot, painting and Suggest wait until you
   pick one. In **another shot** of the take (shots joined by camera moves) you can also paint, or press **New groups
   for this shot**: the camera move between the two becomes a cut, so that shot gets its own groups on the frame on screen
   and its own seed, and the groups at frame N keep seeding theirs (one undo step together with the first stroke; Ctrl+Z
   gives back the take as it was). The best frame to paint and the fill from a repeat look only at the selected shot. Groups are stored
   per scene, so detect scenes first. **Repeated frames**: when the groups' frame shows up again, exactly, in
   other takes (the ruler's repeat ticks), the notes card offers **Copy groups to N repeats**: the same pixels, so the groups go

   an empty take**: select a take without groups with the tool open and it fills by itself when any of its

   frame inside a stored tracker run of another take that was tracked with your groups (the points the tracker had there
   on each part, visible ones only, become that part's seed points: Track seeds exactly those points, drawn as dots over
   their footprint). It prefers your loop frame, then the match nearest the take's start, then painted over tracked, then
   the most parts; the take is seeded on that frame and tracked both ways from it. The notes card says **Groups from m:ss ·
   same frame** or **Points from the run at m:ss** with **Drop** (Ctrl+Z works too); a take filled once or dropped is
   never filled again by itself (the project remembers it). Copies are not new training examples for the seeder (the same
   picture) until you edit the take: then it is yours. Turn it off in the bar's **Options** (fill an empty take from a repeat), or Track › Groups: fill
   empty takes from repeated frames); then **Use groups from m:ss** / **Use points from the run at m:ss** does it on your
   click, also on a repeat tick's right-click menu. A take without groups also shows its **best frame to paint**: the frame
   whose repeats reach the most other takes without groups (each fills from it), then your loop frame, then the most
   repeated, then the earliest (**Go there**; a white marker over the ruler's repeat strip, click it to go there).
2. **Paint first.** Drag on the video: a stroke that starts on a group adds to it, anywhere else it starts a new
   **unassigned** group (`? 1`, `? 2` ..., drawn neutral and dashed). So you can blob in all the parts in a row without
   touching the bar. **Ctrl**+drag always starts a new group, even on top of another (a penis over the balls: paint the
   balls, then Ctrl+drag the penis; after that a stroke starting inside the penis adds to the penis, because a stroke goes
   to the active group when it starts on it, else to the smallest group under it). **Shift**+drag erases the group under the
   pointer (else the active one). The label next to the brush says what the next stroke does (`new group`,

3. **Assign.** **Assign all** (in the groups list, when groups are unassigned) walks through them: the group lights up and a

   `lh` = L hand) or click it; parts the character does not have yet come first. It moves on to the next one; **Esc**

   trade names. The picker's **ignore** makes the group an **ignore region** (a watermark, an inset: the seeder's training
   blacks it out; **V** then a stroke paints one directly); click it again for an unassigned group. Every assignment is one undo step and the group takes its character's color (Bottom blues, Top oranges, Extra greens). The
   **ask** box opens the picker after every stroke that makes a new group, for painting one part at a time. Unassigned
   groups are saved but **not tracked**: Track leaves them out and says how many.

   **thighs**, **shin** (the lower leg), **paws** (called **feet** with View › Furry items off). **L / R is always the character's own left / right**, whatever side of the
   picture it is on. The picker's figure faces you, so their L is on your right (it says *their L* / *their R* under it). A

4. **Paint into one group**: Shift+click a row in the groups list: strokes paint into that group wherever they start. The
   armed group shows as a chip beside the bar; click the chip to go back to paint first.
5. **The brush.** The ring is exactly where the stroke's edge lands (its middle circle; the faint inner and outer circles
   are the soft falloff, which only shows in the tint). A click paints a whole disc, a stroke paints the ring's radius on
   either side of its path however fast or slow you go (every pointer sample is used), painting over a spot again changes
   nothing, a pen's pressure scales the radius. Size: the wheel over the video (Ctrl+wheel still zooms), **[** / **]**, or drag up / down (or scroll) on the
   bar's brush size button. **Edge snap** (the magnet on the bar, off by default; drag up / down on it for its strength; setting `tracking.groups.snap`): scrub a rough blob over a part and the outline you see, while you paint, is already the snapped one: smoothed, drawn onto a close clear color edge of the frame (at most 0.3 × the brush radius, the pull fading out toward that reach and scaled by how clear the edge is against its surroundings; none close: your blob's own smoothed outline), then kept 1 to 1.5 mask pixels inside it so seed points never land on the background (thin shapes are left alone). What you saw is what is kept, in the same undo step; adding to or erasing from a group only reshapes the outline near the stroke. Alt+drag paints one stroke the other way (Alt+click still picks). The tint is what you painted, the outline on top is what the tracker gets (the mask's 50 % edge, to a fraction of a
   mask pixel, at most 64 corners). A group painted in separate patches tracks only the largest: the others are drawn dashed
   gray and the notes card offers **Split into groups** (each patch its own group: `c1_tail`, `c1_tail2`) or **Drop them**.
6. **Fix a group without repainting**: **Alt**+click picks the group under the pointer (again: the next one under it),
   **G** grabs it (move the mouse, click or **Enter** keeps, **Esc** or right click cancels), **S** scales it the same way,
   **X** / **Delete** deletes it, the eye in the groups list leaves it out of Track. Handy for a re-found group that is nearly right.
7. **Suggest** proposes the parts on the frame on screen. The parts the **seeder** has learned from your own painted groups

   5 painted examples from at least 2 videos; the other parts (sheath, slit, anus, tail, paws ...; with Furry items off no tail or sheath is offered; until you have painted
   enough of them) come from the tagger's heat maps as before, outlined by the `sem_shape` param; with `sem_shape: sam` also
   the two largest bodies. Each suggestion shows its **confidence**: a percentage and a bar under its name chip and in the
   groups list (**shown ≥ N** there sets which suggestions show: drag up / down or scroll on it); a learned one under 70 % is **tentative** (faint, short dashes, a `?`). In a take with no painted groups
   and the playhead in its first 2 s, Suggest looks at the take's first 2 s (every 0.4 s and the frame on screen) and
   proposes on the frame that shows the most parts confidently; when that is not the frame on screen the groups land on
   that frame and the notes card says **Suggest picked frame N · 5 parts vs 3 on frame M** with **Go there** (the playhead
   never moves by itself). A frame that repeats exactly elsewhere counts for a little more (its groups can be copied to every
   repeat; up to 1.5 parts for 8 repeats and more) and the loop frame you picked on the ruler for 2 more; the notes card then says
   why (**repeats 12×**, **your loop frame**). Suggest also looks at up to two such frames of the take's first 2 s. Characters: two characters (two clusters of parts, or two hips / heads / penises) are split by
   how the parts look and where they are, every part at most once per character; Bottom is the one that looks like this
   video's painted Bottom in your other scenes, else the one whose head is further left (all its parts when no head was
   found: in contact both hips sit at one place, the heads stay apart); one character stays one. Per suggestion: **✓**
   accepts it (it becomes a painted group), **✕** deletes it (or **X** / Delete), repainting it with the brush, moving /
   scaling it, or giving it another part or character (picker, a dropped label) fixes it and makes it painted too;
   **Accept all** (the ✓ on the bar with the count, or **Enter**) accepts every suggestion of the take. A new Suggest replaces the previous suggestions,
   never a group you painted. Track ▸ Groups: swap Bottom and Top swaps those two characters in every group of the take (Extra stays). Suggest never makes an Extra: paint it yourself.
   A second group of the same part becomes `c1_tail2`. **body** is special: not pinned, its points join the motion groups;
   the others are pinned groups with channels `sem_c1_penis_*`, `sem_c2_hand_l_*` ...

   **The seeder learns from what you paint.** Its labels are the painted groups (and accepted or fixed suggestions) of the
   projects in the **seed library**: the `.pawproj` files in the folders of your recently opened videos and projects (not
   their subfolders), plus the current project; the setting `tracking.seeder.folders` replaces the folder list. After a
   save whose painted groups changed it retrains in the background ("Training the seeder" in the status bar; a Track or a
   Suggest you start meanwhile stops it, and the next save picks up where it left off). The **Seeder** line (the bar's Options) says
   what it learned from (`learned from 29 painted frames · 3 videos`; its tooltip lists the parts it answers for and the
   library), its ↻ button (or Track ▸ Groups: retrain the seeder) retrains now. The first training reads each painted
   frame once through the tagger (on the GPU, the same pass Suggest makes; cached in the app's data folder `seeder\feats`,
   ~2.3 MB a frame); after that a retrain takes seconds on the CPU. The model is ~1.2 MB and runs on the CPU (no more
   VRAM than the tagger Suggest already loads). It is about twice as often right as the tagger alone was (measured on the
   three painted videos: a part found 0.39 vs 0.21 of the time on a video it never saw) and gets better with painted frames
   from more videos: a few frames in each of many videos help more than many frames of one.
8. The bar sits at the stage's left edge, beside the picture (a zoomed view keeps its place under it), turns see-through while a
   stroke passes under it, and the ⌃ button folds it to that one button. **Esc** closes the picker, a grab or a stroke in progress, then the tool. Starting a
   track job closes the tool by itself (a stroke in progress is kept); **G** opens it again where you are. Off, the scene's
   groups stay as faint outlines on the video.

Track (and Track tagged scenes) sends the groups of every scene in the range: there they **replace** the tagger's pinned
groups, and that scene is seeded on the frame they were painted on. Each pinned group gets 10 % of the box's points (at least
6), and all groups together at most half: with many groups every share shrinks evenly to half the box divided by the number
of groups (1024 points: 16 groups get 32 each; the minimum of 6 holds while the box has at least 12 points per group). A body
gets the same share as free points. With groups in the range and nothing drawn, Track uses the whole frame as the box. Every
brush stroke, assignment, trade, move, scale, split, swap, delete, toggle and Suggest is one undo step, and the groups (outline and mask) are saved in
the project with the scene.

After the run your groups show like the seeded ones: lane chips **Bottom penis (drawn)**, **Top L hand (drawn)** ... with
`_proj`, `_rel`, `_scale`; the overlay's Parts layer draws every group with its label in its character's color, and the
Bodies layer marks each character's center (**B**, **T**, **X**) and names the body it sits in. The first hips group keeps the
auto-primary hips rule (a plain hips group, else Bottom's, else Top's). With characters the run also has:

- **Bottom center** / **Top center** / **Extra center** (`who_c1_proj`, `who_c2_proj`, `who_x_proj`): the mean of all the character's pinned groups, anchored at the
  seed (a part dropping out does not move it), camera removed, on the stroke axis.
- **Bottom ↔ Top** (`who_pair_proj`): the distance between the two centers along the stroke axis (larger = farther apart; camera
  moves cancel out); **Bottom penis ↔ Top hips** (`who_p1h2_proj`) and **Top penis ↔ Bottom hips** (`who_p2h1_proj`) where both groups
  exist. Raw distances in frame heights: `who_*_dist`.

They are chips in the Runs lane picker like any other channel (the tracker's own pick never chooses them: pick one when the
relative motion is the stroke, e.g. both bodies move).

## Runs and ghosts

Each run is listed under **Runs** by its title, **S4 · 1:12.3–1:40.0 · #2**: the scenes its range overlaps (`S3–S5` across several, nothing when the project has no scenes), its range, and its place among the runs over the same scenes (by run number; recomputed when scenes are re-cut). Under it: the tracker and the primary it used (`TAPNext++ points · proj_cam`), point count and flips. The ghost takes the title as its name when the run finishes (lane legend, toasts). Things you can do per entry:

- Color dot: show / hide the ghost. Name: select it (its overlay frames and ROI show on the video at the playhead; its trace on the lane brightens).
- **×** removes a run. Pinned ghosts (the reference script imported next to the video) cannot be removed.
- **Signal per scene** (the controls band under the stroke lane): one compact row for the scene's run (the selected run, else the run linked to the current scene): `#n · scene k` (where a pick goes: the timeline's selected scene, else the one at the playhead), the **source** (click: a picker in plain words: **Tracker's choice** and **Fused** first, **Shaft**, the **Parts** (each part's variants in a submenu: Position, Own motion, Size ...), **Pairs**, **Bodies** (each character's center), **Other** (**All points, camera removed** = `proj_cam`, **All points** = `proj`, **Camera left / right** and **up / down** = `cam_x` / `cam_y`); a channel with no data in the shot (or the range override) is left out, and one grayed row at the end counts them), **⇄ Pair**, **vs camera** (what the signal is measured against) and the baseline / envelope. A pick is the scene's (every shot of it), saved on the shots (`derive.source`) and one undo step. **Pair**: a small chip of the run's parts opens; click the part that moves, then the part it moves against: the script comes from their relative motion (the first's center minus the second's on the stroke axis; camera moves cancel), applied at once; hovering the second part previews its trace; **on video…** picks the two by clicking their trackers on the video instead (Esc cancels). **vs …**: Camera (default: the camera's motion removed, as Adjust sets it; its Mode / Strength / Smooth are in the same menu), None (image positions), or a part (the source measured against it: a single part becomes the pair part ↔ it; Fused takes every part's center minus it); no re-track. **A range inside the run**: with an I-O range over the run (or an override segment clicked), the label shows the range and the source / Pair / vs pickers set a **range override** there instead: its own source, its own against and camera removal, for that stretch only. It shows as a marked segment in the band along the lane's bottom edge: drag its ends to resize, click to select it (the pickers then edit it), **X** / **Delete** or its **×** removes it, right-click for its options; its ends cross-fade over 0.3 s into the scene's own signal (mapped onto its range, its sign aligned), so a join never jumps. Overrides live on the scene (`signal.spans`, absolute times, like the Detail overrides) and are undoable. A new run picks its signal per scene from the scene's **camera tag** (Detect scenes), where the scene has no signal of its own yet: pan, zoom and handheld -> `proj_cam` (camera removed); turntable -> **Bottom ↔ Top** (`who_pair_proj`, the two characters' relative motion) when it covers half the scene, else **Fused**, never `proj_cam` (a turntable is no translation); rides the stroke -> `proj` (no camera removal; the `cam x` / `cam y` tips say they are stroke candidates); static -> the tracker's pick. The Why section's first line names the tag ("turntable: camera removal off, rolling axis"). When the run's camera estimate is weak (mean `cam_conf` under 0.3) the `proj_cam`, group and part-position chips are grayed ("camera estimate weak").
  - **Seeded groups** (tracker PROTOCOL v4.6, box runs): the shot tagger finds penis, balls and hips on the seed frame and the tracker gives each a fixed share of the points for the whole scene, kept out of the motion groups. They get chips with a pin and their own colors: **hips (seeded)**, **balls (seeded)**, **penis (seeded)**. Hips and penis pick `sem_<name>_proj` (the group's position on the stroke axis); balls picks `sem_balls_scale` (its contraction, which is what balls are good for). The ▾ offers the others: `_proj`, `_rel` (its motion in the frame of the body around it) and `_scale`. The tip says where the group came from (seeded from the tagger (CAM), the tag score, its points), how much of the scene it is present on, and the decision record's numbers for its `_proj`. A chip present on less than half the scene's frames is grayed, and `_proj` / `_rel` gray out with a weak camera like the other position chips. **Hips are the auto primary when they are there**: on a box run with `primary: auto`, a scene whose seeded hips are visible on at least half its frames (with `sem_hips_proj` defined on at least half) uses `sem_hips_proj`, and the Why line says so ("auto: pinned hips found on 78 % of frames → sem_hips_proj"). On the gold penetration scenes with hips found it beat `proj` on 10 of 15 (F1 0.71 against 0.59). Two clicks on two bodies still pick `dist01`, and an explicit primary always wins.
  - **Relative channels** (built in the app, not by the tracker): one group measured against another, e.g. a penis against the other character's hips. Press **⇄** at the end of the group chips (or Shift+click a group chip), then click a second group chip: the scene's signal becomes `rel:<a>:<b>` and shows as one chip, **penis ↔ hips (seeded)**; Esc cancels the arm. Where both exist, a dashed ready-made chip offers **penis ↔ hips** (the seeded penis against the seeded hips). The value is group a's centroid minus group b's (the camera-removed `_x` / `_y` channels; a camera move cancels out anyway), projected on the stroke axis: the run's custom axis, else the tracker's axis for the scene (the Why section's axis line), else the main direction of the difference over the scene. It is signed, so the two may pass each other, oriented so it is positive on average, then 0..1 per scene by its min..max like `pair`: **closer = deeper** (lower pos), whatever order you clicked. That sign is the distance's own: the tracker's scene sign is not applied; flip a scene (the ± strip, F, or the chip's ▾) for farther = deeper. This is not a part's `_rel`, which is the part's motion in its OWN body's frame. When the two groups are the run's two largest bodies the chip's ▾ also offers the tracker's `pair` (the same two groups on its own axis). The blobs view joins the two centroids with the value on the line, and the Why section says **lane: penis ↔ hips (relative, frontend · axis tracker 90°)**. The pick is saved like any channel (the link keeps the id; the run keeps the four `_x` / `_y` channels and the scene's axis, so it rebuilds after a reload).
- **Stitching**: where a run's scenes come from different channels / fits, they are stitched at each cut after flips. Where the action continues (a scene marked *continues*, its internal-view members, or two scenes of the run inside one document scene) a scene starts on the level the one before ended on (the mean of its stroke midpoints over 2 s either side; with **env** on both, the stroke size too), squeezed to stay in 0–100; a cutaway in between is skipped, so the bridge over it lands on that level. At a real cut each scene keeps its own center. Either way the data-end points on the cut frame go, two turns the same way at the cut merge into the more extreme one, a stroke cut by the boundary gets its missing turn back, and no two points end up closer than the min gap: the join is one stroke-long move, never a jump. Each join of the selected run is drawn dashed.
- **Baseline** (same strip, `off / auto / knots`): a picked signal often rides a slow motion (camera drift, a body bob: half a wave or a whole one over the scene). The baseline is a smooth curve through the middle of the strokes; the scene's points come from the signal minus it, then the usual fit. **auto**: one knot about every 2 s (at least 3, the first and last on the scene's ends), each the local trend of the midpoints between consecutive turns (a top and the next bottom; turns of at least 10 % of the scene's range) within one knot spacing; a Catmull-Rom curve through them. **knots**: your own curve (seeded from auto). The baseline is drawn dashed through the trace with knot dots: drag a dot (half the pointer's speed, Shift finer; the ghost follows while you drag), double-click the curve to add a knot, right-click a dot (or click it, then Delete) to remove it. **env** adds an envelope: top and bottom curves through the turns, and every stroke is stretched between them (full strokes all scene long); auto only. Switching to off / auto keeps your knots for later. It is all stored on the scene's link (project block `links`), never baked; it needs scenes and a picked channel (not the tracker primary). Baseline edits, like channel picks and flips, are not undo steps.
- **Detail**: in the channel fit settings, 0 puts one point at each stroke turn; 1 to 10 also keeps the tracked shape between turns (holds, eases, double bumps) with as few extra points as needed (Douglas-Peucker per stroke, tolerance 12 pos units at 1 down to 1 at 10). Straight strokes never gain points.
- **Bake** (Shift+B) / **Flip** / **Export**, see below.
- Score against the reference: when a reference ghost is loaded, every run is scored by the tracker: F1 (best sign), fixed-sign F1, precision / recall, median timing error, lag, position correlation, amplitude ratio, per scene when there are several.

Ghosts you did not make in this session (the reference, imports, ghosts from a loaded project) are listed too; they can be shown, selected, scored, copied into the lane (`Ctrl+Enter`) and exported, but have no run record (no flips, no channel, no camera check).

### The blobs view

Grid runs (point trackers with motion groups) can be drawn as **blobs** (the video's **View** menu, **Trails** > **Tracker view**): what the tracker sees, layered over the video, to see why a signal looks the way it does. Everything follows the playhead (scrubbing and playback), from the selected run's stored frames, or the live frame while tracking. Every layer has its own switch in the options (all on by default except Hulls and Occluded points).

Every center the view draws (crosshairs, paths, speeds) is the **anchored** one, never the middle of the visible points: the tracker's own center for that group (its `sem_<name>_x` / `who_<c>_x` channel plus the camera; a moving group without a channel: the same anchoring over its points), which is the members' mean position moved by the mean displacement of the ones still visible. A point dropping out (a thumb behind a shaft) does not move it; the hand only moves when the hand moves. Live frames carry no channels, so there the view does the same from the points: a hidden member counts where it was last seen, carried along with its group (for up to 2 s).

- **Heatmap**: a soft field over each group's area in its color (pinned parts in theirs). Each point's glow is sized to its group's point spacing (**Heat size** × the typical distance to the nearest neighbor, 1.5 to 8 % of the frame height), so a dense grid blobs into one surface over the body and a sparse hand still shows as a blob; dense and sparse groups reach the same brightness instead of a dense one burning white. **Heat shows**: `density` (flat: where the points are), `speed` (only what moves), `both` (the default: moving points brighter, still ones dimmer but visible). Static and unassigned points glow dimmer than the moving groups.
- **Points**: every visible point as a dot in its group's color (a part's lighter, static gray, unassigned white); the pinned penis / balls / hips points get a ring; a point that went occluded shows hollow for a few frames.
- **Parts**: the penis, balls and hips groups seeded from the tagger (v4.6) as dash-dot outlines of their points in their own colors (hips violet, balls and penis lighter), labeled with their name and point count (`hips · 12`), their anchored center as a crosshair, and their `_proj` as a small gauge beside it.
- **Bodies**: per moving group (g1 sky, g2 orange, g3 green, g4 pink) its anchored center as a crosshair, a label `g1 · 63 pts · 0.30 h/s` (its number, visible members, speed in frame heights per second) and a velocity arrow (0.25 s of travel). A body's parts get a small crosshair labeled `p1 · 8`. **Center paths**: each center's path, past 2 s solid and fading, next 2 s dashed.
- **Camera**: the camera's motion as an arrow from the frame center (0.5 s of travel), the scene's camera kind (`pan left`, `zoom in`, `handheld`, ...) and `cam_conf`, over a faint grid glued to the background (dimmer when `cam_conf` is low, dashed at 0: no reference, cam held).
- **Primary**: what the tracker picked as the scene's primary, outlined thick and tagged `primary · sem_c1_hips_proj 0.63`: the pinned part, the clicks, or (proj, proj_cam) a crosshair on the whole cloud's center.
- **Axis**: the run's stroke axis through the frame (its custom arrow, else the direction the tracker reports for the scene, e.g. `auto 63°`, else vertical / horizontal, else a PCA estimate, labeled `PCA est.`), a tick where the lane's signal source projects on it with the signal's value.
- **Hulls** (off by default): a filled translucent hull per body, its parts' nested and dashed. Hovering and clicking inside a body or part works either way.
- **Occluded points** (off by default): every occluded point as a small square, and the overlay's boxes. Point elements a newer tracker adds after the pinned group (flags, residuals) draw as a ring per non-zero element, colored by value (0..1: blue to red).
- **Tracker dock** (the slim column on the video's left edge,): one row per channel of the scene on show (pinned parts such as `C1 penis` first, then the motion groups, still and unassigned points; when the run has pinned parts the motion groups fold into one closed **Motion groups** row, a click opens it), its overlay color, a bar for how much it moves on the stroke axis (against the most moving one; camera removed), how closely it follows the run's stroke (`.83`: the correlation with the lane signal over the scene; yellow under .40) and, under the bar, a yellow line for its share of points lost when that is 15 % or more. Hover a row: that part lights up on the video. **Click** a row: hide / show its points, outline and heat (settings `tracking.dock.hidden`); **Ctrl+click**: only that one (again: all). Below the rows, view toggles: points, center paths, part outlines, heatmap, labels (off keeps the hovered / selected / primary thing's), camera, axis + pair, the fusion view and the Info card; the link button picks a pair on the video. The chevron collapses the dock to icons (`tracking.dock.collapsed`); the sliders button opens the options (below).
- **Fusion view** (on by default; while the shot's signal is **fused**, or Smart stroke is on): how each part feeds it. A ring per contributing part, sized by its weight there now (the fusion's per-window weights, cross-faded), with its share in %; its points drawn bigger and the others fainter (runs tracked this session: a run read back from a `.pawruns` file has no per-point part ids); its motion on the stroke axis as an arrow, turned the way the fusion turns it, so parts that agree point the same way and one pulling against the stroke points back; the pairs the fusion uses joined by dashed lines (thicker = more say); at their weighted center the fused result: the weighted arrow and a small 0..1 gauge of the fused value.
- **Pick a pair** (the dock's link button, or the Pair chip under the run): click the moving part, then the part it moves against; that pair becomes the shot's signal. Hover rings the part under the pointer; Esc or right-click cancels.
- **Info card** (off by default; the dock's info toggle, beside the dock): the frame's primary signal (the tracker's per-scene decision, else `auto` resolved through `primary_used`) and value, the tracker's reason for it and its sign decision when it reports them, the lane's channel when it is another, `proj`, `proj_cam`, `dist01`, `cam_conf`, `vis_frac`, `confidence`; a sparkline of t − 2 s .. t + 1 s with the primary bright and the candidates (`proj`, `proj_cam`, `dist01`, `who_pair_proj`) dim in their colors behind it, so you see where they part ways.
- **Hover** inside a body: that group is highlighted (also in the lane) and the Info card adds its member count, center, speed and hull area; inside a part: its member count; inside a pinned part: its points, `_proj`, `_scale` and share. Hover a point: its index, group, part, pinned group, how long it has been visible or occluded, its speed, and its group history through the scene (`g1×120 → –×3 → g1×80`). While hovering, channels the Info card does not know are listed under **other**.
- **Options** (the sliders button on the tracker dock, stored in settings `tracking.blobs.*`): every layer on / off; **Overlay opacity** for the whole view and, under each switch, that layer's own opacity (defaults: Heatmap 70 %, Points 90 %, Parts 100 %, Bodies 100 %, Camera 80 %, Hulls 40 %; the two multiply); **Smoothing** (off, 0.2, 0.5, 1 s: a centerd moving average of the hulls and the centers, display only); **Points shown** (all, 1 in 4, 1 in 9 on a coarse lattice of the seed positions): fewer dots, and hulls that follow the body's area instead of every small fast part.

**Why this signal.** A points run (`cotracker_points`, `tapnext_points`) shows, in the selected run's body, one line per scene: **Primary: proj, auto: 1 click → proj** (what the signal is and the rule that picked it), a **How** sentence (how that signal is computed, e.g. proj_cam: the visible points' mean position, camera removed, projected on the stroke axis, normalized 0..1 per scene), and a closed **Why** section: every primary the run could have used with its numbers (std and travel of the raw signal in frame heights, share of frames with a value, camera confidence for proj_cam, group share for g1–g4, share and presence for a seeded group (`sem_hips_proj · hips (seeded)`); the picked one first), then the axis (its screen angle), the sign decision (keep / auto and the score behind it), the camera (confidence, how much it moved), and low motion / cadence fill / held input when they applied. The camera check runs after every run that is not whole-frame. ### The fused signal: every tracked part at once

A run with painted groups (two or more) offers a **fused** chip, second in the source picker. It combines
every group and every pair across the characters (Bottom part against Top part, and Extra part against Bottom part, never Extra against Top: their relative motion, which a camera
move does not touch) on the stroke axis: each is sign-aligned to their consensus and weighted, per 2 s window, by how
well it agrees with the others there, so a part that is hidden for a while hands over smoothly. Pairs count first;
single parts are tie-breakers; secondary parts (tail, head, paws or feet, hands, torso) start at a fifth of the weight, and a
part that moves a beat behind the others (a tail that lags the hips) keeps a tenth: it never decides the timing. The
signal follows the scene's direction as the tracker picked it; **F** flips it like any other. Each part's slow drift
(over two strokes) is removed, never its stroke size: shallow strokes stay shallow.

When a shot uses it, the shot's **Adjust** (the ▾ of the band under the stroke lane) lists **Fused from**: each part or pair, its share of the signal, and (in its
tip) whether it moves with or against the stroke, how well it agrees and whether it lags. **pin** uses only the pinned
ones, **×** leaves one out (one undo step each, stored on the shot). A thin stacked strip shows who carries the signal
over time, in the parts' colors. New runs that can fuse use it through Smart stroke (below).

### Smart stroke and Strokes: from the signal to points

**Smart stroke: fused + strokes** (the band's ▾, one click, one undo step) gives the shot the fused signal through the
new strokes step. When you switch it on, the points the shot had before stay drawn over it, gray ("Without Smart
stroke"), in the script lane, so you can compare the two and switch back (a shot that arrived with it on shows no gray
line until you switch it off and on). It is on for every newly tracked shot whose run can fuse; off for a shot stays off
(through saving, re-derive and undo), a re-track keeps the shot's choice, and runs tracked before keep what they had.

**Strokes** (same strip) turns the shot's signal into points; touching a control turns it on for the shot (Reset goes
back). It is the tracker's own step, now live: a turn where the signal turns back by at least **Min stroke** of the
local stroke size (3 s window), the stroke lasts **Min length** and is at least a tenth of the shot's median stroke (a
hold's jitter makes no turn); timing to a fraction of a frame (with Smart stroke,
from the parts that agree best at that turn); **Detail** keeps frames between two turns that are more than that many
pos off the straight line (0 = turns only; the point count shows live); **Max speed** shortens a too fast step in depth,
never in time; **Depth**: *scene* (the shot's own range: shallow strokes stay shallow, a held level stays where it
is), *follow* (a slow 8 s envelope), *each* (every stroke full size, the tracker's habit) or *compress* (opt-in: a
compressor for long handheld or POV runs; strokes far above the run's median size are pulled in above a soft knee, so a
few big strokes or moves no longer swamp the rest, and a bigger stroke stays bigger; its curve's two handles set the amount
and how long a center move must last to count as depth). On the tracker's own
pick it starts from the tracker's processed signal (its camera trend removed), so it gives the tracker's turns.
Hover a point's mark on the lane for why it exists (turn, detail, end, clamped). Low-confidence stretches of a
fused signal (its parts disagree for a second or more) are marked "low confidence: Fill" and left empty for Fill or
the bridge. Cadence fill (opt-in, needs the video) stays the tracker's: a re-track applies it.

### Track direction: forward, backward, both

**Track direction** (Tracker panel, under Track; commands **Track forward / backward / both from the seed frame** in the
palette, no keys yet) tracks from the seed frame: the frame the box or the groups were drawn on, else the playhead.
**both** tracks the take both ways from the seed: forward to its end and backward to its start, one run (every tracker:
the points trackers track the buffered take backwards themselves, the others run on the video played in reverse from the
seed frame, then forward; a box drawn on a frame in the middle of a take is used on that frame, no longer on the take's
first frame). **fwd ▶** tracks from the seed to the end of its take, **◀ back** from the seed to the start of its take
(any tracker). From a
shot's first half second, backward goes on past its cut into the take before it (the pan or zoom that arrives at the
action), up to the next hard cut back; forward from a shot's last half second likewise. An I / O range, or a selection
of two shots or more, is the limit instead. Every cut inside is tracked through (one run, no reseed), and the run stops
where its points are lost (under a quarter of the groups' points visible for half a second). While it runs, the lane
shows a band growing from the seed (leftward for backward); the finished run marks its seed. A hard cut it tracked
through is offered as a camera move (a toast with **Camera move**: the shots join one scene, tracked through; one undo
step); decline and the run still stands. Cancel stops the job; a points run only hands its frames over at the end of
each take, so a canceled one keeps nothing yet.

### Adjust after tracking: camera and axis without a re-track

Tracking the points is the slow part; everything after it is maths on what the run stored (the point tracks, the
centroid, every group's center, the camera path, kept in the `.pawruns`). **Adjust · shot N** (the ▾ of the band under the stroke lane, Track tab;
for the selected shot and the run linked to it, else the selected run) changes that maths for the shot, and the ghost, the
lane and a live shot's script follow at once:

- **Camera**: **auto** (as tracked: the tracker's camera path, translation; the camera tag picks the signal as before),
  **off** (no camera removal: image positions, for a camera that rides the stroke), **move** / **zoom** / **rot** (the
  camera re-estimated from the stored points: translation, + zoom, + rotation). **Camera from**: the static points (in no
  body or group) or all points. move / zoom / rot need the point tracks of that scene (a run read from a `.pawruns`, or
  tracked in this session).
- **Strength** 0..100 %: how much of the camera is taken out (partial removal when the camera half rides the stroke).
  While you drag it (or **Smooth**), the lane also draws the same signal with no camera removal, dashed: raw
  beside camera-removed. With **Stabilize** on, the picture shows the same camera removal, zoom and rotation included.
- **Smooth**: a moving average (ms) of the camera path before it is removed, so handheld jitter stays in the signal
  instead of being added to it.
- **Stroke axis (this shot)**: **tracked** (the run's own), **auto**, **vert**, **horiz**, **arrow**: **On video** draws or drags the
  arrow on the picture (press at OUT, release at IN, drag an end to turn it); the ghost follows while you drag.
- **shot / scene**: whether a change reaches this shot or every shot of its scene. **Reset**: back to how it was tracked.
- With a camera override every signal of the shot is camera-handled the same way (`proj` = `proj_cam` there). Direction
  stays the F flip; the signal stays the chip pick.

Each release is one undo step, stored on the shot (saved with the project). A live shot follows it at once; a baked shot
never changes by itself (Unbake gives it back to its run). A run without stored tracks (rebuilt from a project without its `.pawruns`, a box or mask tracker, an older
tracker) says **Re-track needed** and why. In the tracker form, ↻ marks the settings that only apply at the next Track.
Under **Strokes**, a closed **Advanced** section holds the tracker's script-shaping tracker params (`pipe_smooth_ms`,
`pipe_norm_window_ms`, `pipe_min_prominence`, `pipe_hold_ms`, `pipe_rdp_epsilon`, the output range, `pipe_min_interval_ms`,
`pipe_max_speed`, `pipe_snap_frames`, `pipe_norm_floor`, `pipe_cam_detrend`, `pipe_cadence_fill` and the like; not the
activity gate or invert): they moved here from the tracker form, are still the panel tracker's
settings and, marked ↻, apply at its next Track (the run on screen keeps the points it was shaped with). Adjust shows
only while a run covers the shot, so they are reachable once a shot has been tracked; until then the tracker's
defaults (or the values set earlier) apply.

### Direction: flips

Trackers cannot tell deep from out. The **run strip**, the top row of the band inside the stroke lane's bottom edge, shows which run plays where: one **range** per run, in the run's color, with its short name where it fits (the full name in its tooltip).

- Each range on the run strip shows ± : click it to invert that range.
- **F** flips the cut under the playhead (pos → 100 − pos). **Flip from the playhead to the end of the shot** (`track.flipToEnd`, menu Track > Direction and anchors) is the usual fix when the direction turns over inside a shot.
- Drag along a range, or along the empty strip, to mark a stretch: **Invert this stretch**, or **Use <run> here** (offered for every run that tracked all of it). A flipped stretch inside a range is hatched: click selects it, `Delete` removes it, drag an end to trim it; right-click: to the start or the end of the range, **Remove**.
- Click a range to select it (and its run). Drag an end to shorten or lengthen it (on frames, `Shift` free; never past what the run tracked, shown faint while it is selected). `Delete` removes the selected range (the run stays). Right-click a range: **Use another run here**, **Invert this range**, **Bake this range**, **Remove (the run stays)**. Each of these is one undo step.
- **Flip** in the run flips every cut.
- In the **Tracker** row (Refine, Review) a strip under the lane shows one `+` / `−` per scene of the selected run: click it (or **F**) to flip that scene, drag along it to flip a stretch.
- Flips rewrite the run: a live shot's script flips with it, a baked shot keeps its points. **F** and the run's **Flip** are not undo steps: flip again to undo.

Under the run strip, the **range overrides** strip: drag along it to add one (a stretch of the shot with its own source, pair or camera removal); click selects it, drag its body to move it, drag an end to resize it, `X` / `Delete` or its × removes it (a narrow one has no ×: `X`, `Delete` or its right-click menu). Its right-click menu: **Source**, **Measured against**, **Back to the scene's source**, **Whole shot**, **Remove the override**.

### Anchors

On a frame where the script is wrong, **Alt+0** says "fully in here", **Alt+Shift+0** "fully out", **Alt+M** "half way"; the same key on that frame takes the anchor away. Anchors are local: each section of the shot (where the fusion's mix of parts changes) is solved on its own, so a section with anchors takes the mix of parts that meets them, with its level and size set from them, and a section without anchors keeps the automatic result exactly. An anchor where the automatic mix already fits changes nothing, and the toast says so. On a shot that does not play Fused, anchors only set its depth. Put two or three in each bad stretch, not on the good part. The score re-runs after a flip.

### Re-track a range

`tracking.retrack` (menu Track > Runs, ranges and scenes; other panels call it too) uses the current range, switches to the Track tab, sets the range, clears the ROI, seeks to the range start and asks for a new region: draw it and press Ctrl+T (Track ROI).

## From run to script: live and Bake

There is no Accept. A shot with a run and no baked points plays that run's points (**live**): the stroke lane, the Machine, the device, Publish and Export all read them, and every flip, anchor, source pick or Adjust change shows at once. Fills, bridges and holds over shots with no baked points play live the same way.

| Command | Keys | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `tracking.bake` | Shift+B | **Bake**: copy the live points into the axis for good: the selected shots, else the I/O range, else the shot under the playhead. No run, fusion or derive change moves them after that |
| `tracking.bakeRun` | – | bake every shot the selected run plays live |
| `tracking.bakeAll` | – | bake the whole script |
| `tracking.unbake` | – | **Unbake**: remove a shot's baked points, so its run plays there again (same scope as Bake) |

Each is one undo step. A hand edit (a digit key, a drag, a paste) on a live shot bakes that shot with the edit, in the same undo step; live shots it leaves alone stay live. The Scenes row's line under a shot is solid when it is baked, dashed when it is live. Old projects: shots accepted before are baked as they were; a shot that only had an un-accepted run now plays it.

Typical loop: select the shot, track, flip until the direction matches the picture, anchor where the depth is off, next shot. Bake only what you want fixed.

### Effects: a layer over the tracked motion

The thin **Effects** row under the Scenes row holds effects: knotting (with its push and pull), orgasm, throb, idle and **Reshape**. They never change the tracked run: every tracker ghost is drawn through them (the lane shows the run with its effects), and they apply over live and baked points alike. Mute an effect and it is gone from the ghost; delete it and nothing is lost but the effect. Effects are layers: they may overlap, and they apply in time order, the later one on top (a Reshape under a knot, say); overlapping blocks stack in sub-rows. Each one adapts to the motion under it, from the run's own turning points:

- **Knotting**: a knot on every tracked stroke (the fitted knot-pop stroke: pull against the knot, pop out, top, back to the knot, press, pop in), its bottom on each tracked bottom and its top on each tracked top. That is mode **Cycles**. Every mode uses the same knot levels: full depth 0, the catch 30 (where the knot meets the ring going in), the knot depth 20 (where it pops in), the tie 17 (where it rests and pops out), pop out to 64, top 80. Mode **One knot** is one knot going in. It starts from the trace's level at the effect's start (or the *from* you drag), moves to the catch, presses slower to the knot depth, pops to full depth in one frame, drifts up to the tie, and then pulls out: from the tie through 64 back onto the trace at the effect's end. Set the pull-out to 0 and it holds at the tie instead. The defaults come from a hand-made reference knot script: the push takes 49 % of the span with the press its last 17 % (2.6 times slower than the approach), the drift 27 %, the pull-out the last 20 %. The pop takes one frame; on a device too slow for it, the pop keeps its time and lands short. Trackers lose this move: it has no turning points and its slow trend gets normalized away. The effect redraws the span instead, whatever the trace does there. Its handles: the start level, where the push starts, the catch and the press length, the knot depth, the pop's time, the full depth, the drift, the tie, the pull-out length and the level it passes. Mode **Unknot** is the mirror: the knot coming out. It holds at the tie, then pulls slowly to the catch (`tugs` small slips back in by `tugAmp` on the way, 2 by 4 by default, 0 = a straight pull), pops out onto 64 in one frame, and settles onto the end level `to` (100, or the trace at the effect's end if you clear it), held to the end. Its handles: the tie, where the pull starts, two tug diamonds (up–down = how far each slips back, left–right = how many: further left = more, far right = none), the catch, the pop's time and where it lands, the settle and the end level. Mode **Push** is one slow move with no pop (it replaced the Insertion and Pull-out kinds): from the trace's level at the effect's start (or the *from* you drag) eased onto the level it ends at, arriving at 90 % of the span after a move of 85 %, held there to the end. A push ends at full depth (0), a pull at 100; drag the end level anywhere (above the start: a pull). Make one with **Push** or **Pull** in the Effects row's and the Scenes row's right-click menus, or `timeline.mod.add.push` / `timeline.mod.add.pull` in the palette (the range, else the scene). A push under another knotting is a layer like any other: both act. Pick the mode on the popover's **Cycles / One knot / Unknot / Push** switch. A new Knotting effect stores its mode when you make it, and dragging its ends never changes it: Cycles over 3 tracked strokes or more (or over 2 turns or more past 8 s), else Unknot when the span has no turn and the trace is deeper at its start (coming out), else One knot. An effect from an older project (or one marked over more than 8 s before tracking) gets its mode from the strokes under it, and keeps that mode once you drag it. **Fit** in Unknot reads the pull and pop timing the same way (the pop searched after the first third of the move). **Fit** reads the push and pop timing off the run's samples under the effect, but only when they clearly show a push; levels stay as they are. With the Machine panel open, **Machine** fits the push, the press length, the knot depth and the tie so that the Machine's simulated knot (its toy, experience and stroke window) goes in when the effect pops, as one undo step; a toast says how close it got (a toy or receiver that cannot take the knot says so instead). The popover and the effect's block (a small warning triangle; the tooltip says why) also warn when the Machine says the knot would not go in with the timing as it is ("needs 0.8 s more held deep"). To use it, mark where the knot goes in on the scene's knotting tag (right-click the chip: start / end at the playhead), then **+ Knotting effect** on the quick bar (a span of 8 s or less over fewer than 3 strokes starts in One knot), then drag the handles or press Fit. Treat Fit as a starting point, not a measurement.
- An untracked scene under a Push or a One knot gets that move generated (from where the scene before left the script): the push, or the knot going in and held at the tie. **Fit** in Push reads the move's timing off a tracked run under it, when there is one.
- **Orgasm** is a container: a depth band, a tremor bed and its spurts. The tracked turn times stay and the depth is pulled into the band (deep end 0, ceiling 40). On top lies the tremor fitted from the reference orgasms: a deep-end zig-zag (0, 10, 5, 15, 10, 20 … back to 0) every 333 ms, `tremor` deep (20), added from the deep end and never past the ceiling, so it plays at the bottom of every stroke and on a deep hold, where the old flutter did nothing. On a slow device its peaks stay (a 0–20 pulse every 333 ms). It eases in and out over half a stroke at its edges; how it builds and fades is the strength envelope (below). **Base** (0–100 %, default 50) decides how much of the tracked strokes stays: 100 keeps them, 0 holds the trace at its center line (its mean over one stroke), because under an orgasm the tracked strokes may be the body shaking the tracking rather than strokes; 50 leaves them at half, a 20-pos move in the 0–40 band. Its **spurts** are markers that belong to it: every spurt inside its span plays the orgasm's spurt shape (swing, rise, fall, half-swings, decay: the popover's **Its spurts** curve) at its own **velocity** (0–2, default 1; a marker's popover has the one velocity pill). They are drawn on the orgasm's own block on the Modifiers row and move and go with it. With the orgasm selected, **U** places a marker at the playhead and keeps the orgasm selected, so you can tap along with playback; with the playhead outside it, U grows the orgasm to the playhead (and 500 ms past it) and places the marker, one undo step. In the **Track tab** the band under the stroke lane shows **orgasm · N spurts** while an orgasm is under the playhead; unfold the band for its curve, its spurts and its strength (the same popover as in Refine), U adds a marker and **X** with the stroke lane focused removes the marker nearest the playhead (within 300 ms). What the orgasm plays feeds the fusion settings below it.
- **Reshape**: the path between every two tracked turns is re-drawn from one stroke profile: ease leaving and arriving at a turn, a dwell at the top and at the bottom, the depth (lo / hi), and, only if you drag its handle, where the top falls in the stroke (the deep turns stay, the tops move; until then the turns are as tracked). A new Reshape is **fitted from the motion**: the mean shape of the run's own track (its samples, or the scene's picked channel) between its keyframes sets the eases, the dwells and the depth, so a square-ish or eased motion is detected and then adjustable. The fit never moves a turn. **Fit** in its popover does it again.
- **Throb**: a heartbeat on the trace over the span, one-sided and relative to the motion like a spurt. Every `period` ms (667 = 90 bpm) the trace dips `amount` (10) deeper in `attack` ms (40), comes back in `release` ms (290) and holds until the next beat: the shape measured on the owner's own throb (100 → 90 in about 42 ms, back in about 290). **Swell** (the popover switch) throbs outward instead. `double` adds the lub-dub: the first return is cut to +120 ms and a second dip of `double` × amount follows. The beats build and fade with the strength envelope; `jitter` (0–20 %) varies the period, seeded per effect. Each beat is added onto the trace: every tracked point stays, at the trace plus the beat there, squeezed near 0 / 100. **Base** (0–100 %, default 0) first decides how much of the tracked strokes under the span stays: 0 holds the trace at its center line, so the heartbeat reads on its own (on a throbbing scene the tracked motion is usually the throb itself), 100 keeps the strokes with the beats on top; a beat longer than the period finishes before the next. Its curve shows three beats on a flat line: the dip pill, the attack and release diamonds, the period pill (drag the second beat; the label also gives bpm), and the lub-dub pill. **Fit** sets the period from the run's balls / penis scale-channel peaks under the effect (those pulses are the throb), else from the small dips of its track; the amount stays. **One-shot**: a throb can also be a point, like a spurt: **Shift+U** drops ONE beat with its dip on the playhead's frame (attack, amount, release, and the lub-dub if `double` is set; no period, envelope or jitter), spliced into the trace the same way (relative, squeezed near 0 / 100). It follows the throb one-shot before it like spurts do (same scene, else within 10 s, when that one is linked or tuned); **Alt+Shift+U** drops a plain one; Duplicate puts the next one 300 ms on. On the row it is a small diamond in the throb color; its popover shows the single beat (amount pill, attack / release diamonds, dub pill). A throb chip dropped on the row, or **+ Throb** on a throbbing span, is still a span.
- **Orgasm / throb workflow**: on an `orgasm` or `throbbing` scene, U places the cumshots (or the balls button below places them at the contraction peaks), + Throb lays the heartbeat over the throbbing span, Shift+U adds single beats where you see one. A scene tagged **exposed** (dick out, no act) is tracked for the throb: Track tagged scenes runs a points tracker on the whole-frame grid there (reason "exposed: track for the throb (penis/balls scale)"), so the seeded penis / balls groups exist; `sem_penis_scale` / `sem_balls_scale` are what Throb's Fit reads. Autorange rests it at 85–100 and the Throb sits on top.
- **Spurt**: a point, not a span: one cumshot at the exact frame. Press **U** while watching (or stepping frames) and a spurt lands on the current frame; press again for the next one. A spurt rings **around the trace**, it does not slam to a depth: from the motion it swings `amount` (25) deeper, on the frame, then `amount` × `decay` shallower than the motion, then deeper again, … for `count` half-swings (3: deep, out, deep), each held `hold` ms, and settles back onto the motion. A swing deeper takes `rise` ms, one shallower `fall` ms, added onto the trace (every tracked point under it stays; **Base**, default 100, can hold the trace at its center line there instead). Near 0 or 100 a swing is squeezed to the room there, never clipped. An older project's absolute spurt `depth` reads as the default amount. Spurts a few hundred ms apart give the burst train with the video's own timing. **Spurts from the balls** (a suggestion, never automatic): when the scene at the playhead has a balls contraction channel, the Effects row shows a quiet **balls contraction peaks (seeded) → place spurts** button. When it cannot (on a scene tagged orgasm or throbbing, and on any scene while the Effects row is focused or hovered) a grayed **balls → spurts** button shows instead, with a short hint beside it and the reason in its tip; clicking it puts the reason on the status line: *no run under this scene*, *balls seen on 12 % of frames only* (the seeded group is present on fewer than 25 % of the scene's frames, from `sem_balls_n`), *run predates seeded groups (PROTOCOL < 4.6)*, *no balls group in this run: re-track with the current tracker (seeded groups)*, or *no balls in this scene*. The same entry is on the scene's right-click menu (**Place spurts at the balls contraction peaks**, grayed with the reason when off) and in the palette (`fx.spurtsFromBalls`, the reason as its hint); those two place the spurts straight away (one undo step). The source is the seeded balls group's `sem_balls_scale` (a v4.6 box run) when it has values in the scene, else a tracked part named `balls` with a `_scale` channel (a v3.4 points run with group labels on); the button then says which part (`(g1p1)`). Hover or click it to see hollow dashed diamonds at the peaks of that contraction over the scene; then **place** drops a spurt on each (one undo step; frames that already have a spurt are skipped), **×** cancels, and **contraction / swelling** flips which peaks it takes if the diamonds sit between the shots. Peaks: local maxima of the contraction (3-frame mean) above the scene's median + 2 × its robust spread, at least 300 ms apart (the stronger wins). On the row a spurt is a small diamond: drag it to nudge, right-click for the same menu; its curve shows the ringing about a flat line with a pill for the swing (±amount) and diamonds for rise and fall (count, decay and hold under **#**). **One recipe over a range**: outside every orgasm, U links a new spurt to the spurt before it (in the same scene, else within 10 s) when that one is linked or has params of its own, so tune the first spurt and the rest follow it as you place them; **Alt+U** drops a plain one at the defaults. Inside an orgasm a spurt is its marker and never linked: the orgasm holds the one shape.
- **Detect cumshots** (proposals, never automatic). The orgasm workflow: **mark the orgasm** (a span on the Modifiers strip: drag it, or Shift+O at its start and again at its end) → **detect cumshots** (right-click the span: Detect cumshots over this orgasm, or detect cumshots with the playhead in it: it runs over that span) → check → **place spurts** → adjust. "Orgasm" is the span you mark; **spurts** (U) are the markers inside it, one per cumshot on its frame. A spurt placed outside every orgasm span offers, quietly, to grow the nearest orgasm span to it or mark one around it. On a scene tagged orgasm the Effects row shows **detect cumshots**; the scene's right-click menu (**Detect cumshots**) and the palette (`timeline.cum.detect`: the selected range, else the scene at the playhead) do the same on any scene or range, tag or not. It runs the shot tagger over the scene (a status-bar task; Esc cancels) and proposes one spurt time per onset it finds: the spurt tags (ejaculation, cumshot …) and the tagger's cum heat rising, new white fluid pixels near the penis, and, when a tracker run of the scene has a balls contraction channel, its peaks as pulses that strengthen an onset within 100 ms. The proposals are kept on the scene (saved with the project) and drawn on the Effects row as ghost diamonds: **solid = strong** (score ≥ 0.5), **hollow dashed = weak**; hover one for its score. The bar shows how many are left to place; **all / strong only** hides the weak ones, **place spurts** puts a marker on each shown one, its velocity from its score (0.5 at score 0, 1 at 0.5, 1.5 at 1; one undo step; a frame that already has a spurt is skipped); one outside every orgasm grows the nearest orgasm in its scene to it, else an orgasm is marked around it, **↻** detects again (replaces the scene's proposals), **×** drops them (placed spurts stay). Then nudge, delete or add spurts as usual (U). On the gold set the proposals do not follow the reference scripts' orgasm pulses better than chance (in the source code: trackers/README.md, "Cumshot proposals"), so treat them as a starting point to check by eye, not as timing.
- **Spurts from contractions** (pulse groups). In an orgasm the anus visibly contracts once per spurt. Paint it with **Groups** and assign it **anus** (Bottom / Top): its chip gets a **pulse** glyph (on for an anus; click it on a balls, sheath or penis group to use theirs too, click again to turn it off). Then **detect cumshots** (the CumBar, or right-click the orgasm span) also tracks each pulse group with SAM over the orgasm span plus a second on either side (never the whole video; the button's hint says what it uses and how many seconds SAM runs, e.g. *uses anus (C2), balls (C1) · SAM on 24 s*). Each contraction is a cue next to the tagger's: a proposal's hover lists its evidence (*anus (C2) contraction 0.70 · new fluid 0.30*), cues that agree raise the score, and a proposal backed by a contraction sits on the drawing where the contraction appears. The contraction signal lands on the scene's tracker run as a channel (**sem_c2_anus_pulse**: pick the anus chip in the lane's signal picker) so you can see the pulses against the video. Without SAM (or when it loses the region) a cheaper signal from the region's own texture is used instead (the toast says so). Not yet measured against hand-placed spurts on real footage.

**Effects over the stroke's own points.** Effects also act on the script's own points (live shots, baked shots, hand-made points): over their reach the lane shows the stroke through the effects, updated at once on every effect or point change, so a spurt from U is visible straight away. What the effects change is held to the device profile picked in Review (its max speed and min interval, not the lanes' speed color top): one limiter runs after every effect, keeps the times and lowers the moves that are too fast, and drops the point of a too-close pair that moves less, so a spurt's ringing on a Launch is smaller, not later. The points themselves stay as they are until **Flatten** (`timeline.flatten`, "Flatten: write what plays into the points") writes what plays into them, one undo step. Effects that went into the axis are marked **baked**: drawn hollow, they no longer apply (so nothing doubles); they stay parametric, right-click **Un-bake** makes one live again.

**Knotting** and **Spurt** are the two first-class effects (Knotting: **Alt+N** over the knotting span at the playhead, else the range, else the scene; every other way to add a Knotting effect, the quick bar, **+ Effect**, the Effects and Scenes row menus (at the click) and the palette, uses the same rule, and **+ Effect** has **Unknotting** next to it, the last 3 s of that span; Spurt: **U**); the others (Reshape, Orgasm pulses, Throb) are under **More effects** and are no longer offered inline, but old projects' ones render, edit and bake exactly as before (the insertion scene type is unchanged: its push is generated). Add one with **+ Effect** in the Scene chip popover: click a kind for an effect over the scene (or the range; a spurt goes on the playhead's frame), or drag the kind onto the Effects row to add it over the scene under the pointer (a spurt: at the pointer; on top of any effect already there). Right-click the row for the same list. On the row: drag a block to move it, its edges to resize (snaps to cuts, effect edges, the range and the playhead; Shift no snap; other effects never move), click it for its curve, right-click for Curve / Mute / Duplicate / Kind / Delete; Delete removes the selected one (row focused), Enter opens its curve, Esc clears.

**Linked effects.** Effects of one kind can share their params: Shift+click effects on the row (or Shift+drag over empty row space to box-select), right-click, **Link effects**. Each kind in the selection becomes one group (a mixed selection links per kind), taking the params of the effect you right-clicked when it has its own, else of the earliest one that has (a tuned recipe is never replaced by the defaults). From then on an edit of any member's curve, Fit or mode goes to all of them, live while you drag; their spans and times stay their own. A thin line along the top of the row joins a group, and the popover header says **linked ×5 · unlink**. Duplicate keeps the link; changing an effect's kind takes it out of its group; **Unlink** (right-click, or the header) leaves every effect with the params it has. A baked member stays baked when another member is edited (only the edited effect un-bakes, as any edit does). Links are saved with the effects (`link` on each, optional), one undo step per link, unlink or edit.

The curve popover is direct manipulation, no form: Reshape shows one stroke between two turns, Orgasm its flutter with the depth (deep end and ceiling) as two horizontal pills on the same plot, Knotting one knot cycle with its levels (in One knot: the knot going in over the effect, with the push, catch, press, pop, drift, tie and pull-out handles; in Push: the start and end levels, the move's start and its arrival). Under it, every span effect has the one **Strength over the span** envelope (how it builds and fades); no effect has a build or fade of its own. A handle's shape says how it moves: a vertical pill left–right, a horizontal pill up–down, a diamond slides along the curve (an ease). Drags move the handle at half the pointer's speed, Shift finer; the lane ghost follows while you drag and the release is one undo step. A focused handle takes the arrow keys (5 steps, Shift 1, PageUp/PageDown 20, Home/End). **#** shows the numbers (the handles' values and a few more).

### Export a run

**Export** writes the ghost's points next to the video as `<name>.funscript`, or `<name>.pawscript.funscript` when a `<name>.funscript` already exists. "Overwrite the original .funscript (keeps a .bak)…" asks first, copies the current file to `.bak` and keeps its other keys. The reference ghost cannot be exported.

## Scenes, describe, loop

The **Scenes** panel (left dock of the Scenes tab, `Ctrl+1`) has **Detect**, **Describe all** and **Loop** (the selected scene, else the one at the playhead) at the top, with the running job's progress bar, `scene 3 of 6 · 43 % · 1:20 left` and the elapsed time. Below, one row per scene: its number, start–end, duration, the camera glyph (pan, zoom, turntable, handheld, rides the stroke), its tags as chips (the act as words, then view, modifiers, structure; brighter = changed by hand), the tagger's three strongest labels with their scores, and its run status: **not tracked**, **live** (a tracker run covers it and plays there) or **baked** (the active axis has permanent points over at least half of it), with the share covered. Click a row: the scene is selected, the range set to it, its points selected and the playhead at its start, the same as a click in the Scenes row. The selected row opens: its tag chips (the quick bar's, with the suggestions), **Describe** / **Describe again** and **Loop** for that scene, the describe tags that have a box (**Show**, **ROI**) and the loop result. **Scene options** at the bottom (closed) holds every scene setting: scene detection (`cut_*`), loop (`loop_min_confidence`: its only home now; Track sends it too, for `loop_fold: auto`) and describe.

**Detect** (`track.detectScenes`) runs the cut detector over the whole video and fills the scene list (also `app.doc.scenes`, saved in the project, editable on the timeline). Its options are the `cut_*` params above. Besides hard cuts it finds fades to black (or white): the black stretch and its fades become one **Blank** scene (filled black on the scenes row), so the action scene ends where the fade-out starts. Dissolves cut in their middle, a flash frame never cuts, and a still picture of 2 s or more is marked `still` (a chip on the scenes row; at the very start or end it becomes the intro / outro). A fade or dissolve at a scene's edge draws a small ramp (fade) or cross (dissolve) there; the hover text says *fade out 0.6 s*. With the Scenes setting **detect**, Track uses the same detector over its range first. With scenes:

- A row in the Actions panel, "Scene at playhead" (Tracking panel), `track.rangeToScene`: range = that scene, playhead at its start.
- **Loop** (per scene): looks for a repeat. Result line: `exact loop: 24 f / 1000 ms, conf 0.93, 5 cycles`, `periodic, not exact: …`, or `no clear period`; `· drawn on 2s` when the footage is held on twos. Loop option: `loop_min_confidence` (0.5). Exact loops are what `loop_fold: auto` folds, and their cycle starts are drawn as marks on the run's trace.
- **Describe** (per scene) and **Describe all** (`track.describeAll`): the shot tagger. Its labels become the scene's tags (see the workflows guide, "Scene tags"); the row shows the three strongest with scores. A tag with a box has **Show** (draw it on the video inside that scene) and **ROI** (ROI = that box, range = that scene; switches to `sam2_mask` or another box tracker if the current one takes no box). The strong tags of the scenes a range touches are sent with the next Track; the tag `knotting` enables `sam2_mask`'s knot events. Describe options: `frames` (5), `confidence` (0.5; lower = more tags), `box_thresh` (0.5), `seqlen` (1024), `localize` (true). "Describe is not installed" means the `describe` extra or a CUDA GPU is missing. Describe all sends every scene in one request (the tagger loads once; each scene's tags appear as soon as it is done, the progress reads `scene 3/6 · 43 %`); Esc stops it there, scenes already described keep their tags, the rest are not started.

While a track, describe or loop runs, its progress shows in the panel, in the status-bar task and on the timeline: the scenes row sweeps the job's scene (or the whole range of a multi-scene track) in the scene's act color with `43 % · 1:20 left` (the ruler band shows the same sweep, and the words when there are no scenes), and the scene quick bar repeats the words while its scene is being tracked. Time left comes from a smoothed rate over the tracker's progress events (at least one a second); before the first frame you see the tracker's own line or its stage (`starting · model load`, `loading the model`, `waiting for the GPU`), and after the frames `computing the scene` / `motion groups` / `making points`.

A describe with no progress for `tracking.describeTimeoutS` seconds (setting, default 180) is killed and reported as failed, usually the GPU is busy elsewhere. The panel shows "waiting for the GPU" and the elapsed time while it runs.

A Track with no progress for `tracking.stallTimeoutS` seconds (setting, default 600; four times that when it runs on the CPU) is stopped and reported as failed. Esc still cancels at any time. When a GPU tracker runs on the CPU (no usable graphics card), the progress reads `running on CPU: slow`. A graphics card this build cannot run (the tracker tries a few small GPU operations once at start; a newer card that passes keeps the GPU) is named once in a note at the top of the Tracking panel and in the Track menu; the trackers that need a GPU are grayed out in the tracker pickers and the CPU trackers work as usual. A graphics driver error during a job fails that job with "the tracker restarts on the next run": the next job starts a fresh tracker. A job that would need far more graphics memory than is free is refused up front, naming the tracker settings to lower.

## Span labels (derived from the tags)

Track tells the points pipeline what each stretch is (the tracker's `spans`, overriding its own motion gate). The labels are no longer set by hand: Track derives them from the scene tags of the range every time it runs.

| Scene tag | Label | Points become |
|---|---|---|
| structure **intro** / **idle** / **after** / **outro**, or a **Blank** view | `idle` | a hold at the position at the span start |
| **Off-screen** on a scene that is part of a continuation (it continues, or is a cutaway inside one) | `carry` | strokes at the rhythm of the nearest active stretch |
| modifier **orgasm** (over its span inside the scene) | `climax` | points as tracked (the fast strokes kept), only flagged |
| anything else | none | as tracked: the motion gate only flags still stretches |

Old projects may still hold manual labels (settings `tracking.spans:<video>`): they are still sent and win where they overlap a derived one, and the Tracker row (Refine, Review) still draws them. Setting them is left to the hidden commands `track.span.active` / `idle` / `carry` / `climax` / `orgasm` and `track.spans.fromPlan` (palette and scripts; no keys, no menu).

The gate's flags (`idle` at the range ends, `still` inside) and the labels (`idle`, `carry`, `climax`, `orgasm`) show as bands on the selected run in the Tracker row (Refine, Review). `sam2_mask`'s knot events (`knot in` / `knot out`) are instants, drawn as marks, not bands. A flag band is a low-motion stretch kept as tracked: label it `idle` or `carry` and re-track if it should hold or carry. An `orgasm` label is a flag too: the Orgasm modifier shapes it.

## GPU notes

- Install the ML extras once: `uv sync --project trackers --extra sam2 --extra describe` (add `--extra tapnext --inexact` for the point tracker). Needs an NVIDIA driver ≥ 580 and a GTX 16xx / RTX 20xx or newer (RTX 50xx included); torch is the CUDA 13.0 build. Checkpoints download on first use (`trackers/.cache/`, `trackers/.models/`): SAM 2.1 small 184 MB, the tagger ~1.1 GB, TAPNext++ 2.5 GB per size.
- One tracker process at a time; every request starts a new one and reloads its model (SAM 2.1 small: ~1.3 s before the first frame).
- VRAM: the tracker keeps models loaded between runs and caps itself at what stays loaded plus the run's own need (`sam2_mask` max(3, 2 + 0.004 × `chunk_frames`) GB, describe 3 GB, the trackers their measured needs), unloading models the run does not use when the card has no room; `PAWTRACK_VRAM_GB` overrides the budget, `PAWSCRIPT_GPU_FRACTION` (set by `trackers/gpu_lock.py`) wins over both. Nothing spills into system memory: when a run still does not fit, it says what was loaded and what it needed, lower `chunk_frames` or the points, or use a smaller `model`.
- Speed (RTX 4090, 1080p): `sam2_mask` tiny / small ~34 fps, base_plus 29, large 18. TAPNext++ 256 chunk 8 ~25 fps, 512 ~6 fps.
- Describe and the GPU trackers share the card: a describe stuck at "waiting for the GPU" is usually another job holding it.

- **The resident tracker.** The app keeps one tracker process running (`pawtrack serve`) instead of starting one per job, so a model loaded once stays loaded: the first describe of a session pays about 8 s (Python, CUDA and the tagger loading), every later one about 0.2 s per scene, and a re-track with the same tracker skips its model load. It holds one GPU model kind at a time (a track unloads the tagger, a describe unloads the tracker, so VRAM never exceeds what that one job needs), unloads every model after 2 minutes without a job (`PAWTRACK_IDLE_UNLOAD_S`), and the process itself exits after 10 minutes idle (`PAWTRACK_SERVE_IDLE_S`), when the window closes, or with the app (a `tauri dev` rebuild included). Cancel asks the job to stop at its next step and keeps the models; a job that does not stop within 5 s gets the process killed (the next job starts a fresh one). If the resident cannot start, jobs run one process each as before; `PAWTRACK_SERVE=0` forces that.
